## 1. How many detections would chance produce?

For each of the 13 detectable cases, the chance of being detected by chance is 1 − product of (1 − the group's alert rate in the year), over the years of the affected period that are in the data, using the alert rates observed in study 1 (companies in alert ÷ evaluated companies). The number of detections by chance follows the Poisson binomial distribution, computed exactly, with no approximation.

In all 13 cases, the years of the affected period in the data are the same fiscal years evaluated in study 1.

**Result.** Expected detections by chance: 8.56 (standard deviation of 1.61). Detections observed in study 1: 7 of 13. Probability of 7 or more detections by chance: 90.0%.

**Assumptions and what they do to the result.**

- Independence across the years of the same case. If alerts repeat at the same company, this assumption overstates chance: the chance of at least one alert over several years is lower than computed with independent years. So 8.56 is a ceiling for the expected detections by chance. Section 2 measures the repetition, and section 3 redoes the calculation without this assumption.
- Independence across cases.
- Each case has its group's average alert rate in the year. Ground-truth companies may differ from the average (for example, smaller or more leveraged), and their chance rate may be higher or lower than the average; the direction of the effect is not known.
- The rates are study 1's own, which include the ground-truth companies.
- This calculation does not replace the pre-registered metrics (detection and false alarms per detected case), and it was not decided before seeing the result.

## 2. Do alerts repeat at the same company?

Persistence: among company-years in alert whose company was also evaluated in the previous year, in the same group, the fraction that was also in alert in the previous year. The comparison is with the overall alert rate (company-years in alert ÷ evaluated company-years, 2010 to 2023). Each company counts once per fiscal year, as in study 1's rates.

| Group | In alert, with the previous year evaluated | Also in alert in the previous year | Persistence | Overall alert rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Non-financial companies | 2,775 | 2,047 | 73.8% | 49.9% |
| Financial companies | 30 | 14 | 46.7% | 12.0% |
| Total | 2,805 | 2,061 | 73.5% | 48.3% |

**Result.** Among non-financial companies, 73.8% of company-years in alert were also in alert in the previous year, against an overall alert rate of 49.9%. Alerts repeat at the same company, and section 1's assumption of independence across years overstates chance.

## 3. Variant without the assumption of independence across years

"With data" means evaluated in study 1 (at least one computable alert signal) in each of those years. The repetition of alerts at the same company enters the calculation as it happened. The number of detections by chance again follows the exact Poisson binomial distribution.

## 4. Later note: unfilled balance sheets in study 1

**Later note, of 2026-11-08.** Check requested in the reply to Phase B (item 1c). It does not change study 1: it only reports.

The v2 "not filled in" check (`chiocca.v2.conferencias`) found 104 balance sheet columns with zero total assets in the study 1 base: 16 in the fiscal-year column (ÚLTIMO) and 88 in the prior-year column (PENÚLTIMO), in 93 company-years. Of these company-years, 57 are in the non-financial sample, 2 in the financial sample and 34 outside the sample. In 78 of the columns, the whole balance sheet is zero; in the other 26, only the total.

Criterion: a study 1 calculation used zero as a value when the signal has a value in study 1 and at least one balance sheet account it uses, in one of these columns, is zero.

**Result: yes.** In 57 company-years, at least one study 1 calculation used a balance sheet zero from one of these columns as a value.

| Signal | Company-years computed with zero | Alerts from this signal in these company-years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DSRI, days' sales in receivables index | 1 | M-score component: does not trigger alerts by itself |
| DEPI, depreciation index | 2 | M-score component: does not trigger alerts by itself |
| DPO jump | 2 | 0 |
| implied interest rate minus average CDI | 49 | 29 |
| accruals to average total assets | 56 | context only: does not trigger alerts |

- Implied interest rate: in 49 company-years, debt (current plus non-current) is zero in one of the columns. The average debt of t and t−1 is half the other year's debt, and the implied interest rate is double what it would be with that other year's debt.
- Company-years in alert in study 1 only because of signals computed with zero: 29. Company-years evaluated only because of signals computed with zero: 50.
- DPO jump: 2 values computed with zero entered the top-decile threshold of the year. Without them, no DPO alert of another company-year would change.
- Ground truth: none of these company-years is in a case's affected period. Study 1's 7 detections do not change.

In the product, the rule is different: an indicator that depends on an unfilled balance sheet is "not computable", never computed with zero (`chiocca.v2.linhagem.aplicar_nao_preenchido`). The list of company-years is in `resultados/nao_preenchidas_estudo_1.csv`, outside git.
