# Study 2: draft plan

Draft of 2026-11-15, requested by Roberto, for decision. **It is not the pre-registration.** It was written before any study 2 code and without touching any value from fiscal years 2024 onward. The numbers come from [`docs/estudo_2_numeros.json`](../estudo_2_numeros.json), generated by `python -m chiocca.v2.estudo2_numeros`: for fiscal years 2024 and 2025, metadata only (how many companies filed the DFP and when, read from the index of each CVM file, without downloading the statements); the rest comes from study 1 and from fiscal years 2010 to 2022, inside the seal.

What is already decided and does not change here: the seal (`ULTIMO_EXERCICIO_LIBERADO = 2023` and a maximum date of 2024-12-31), the monitor with metadata only for what was filed after 2024-12-31, engine v1 unchanged as the first test (roadmap), no public list ordered by alert, and "alert, not accusation".

Each section brings the options, what each one allows concluding and my recommendation. The list of decisions is at the end.

## a. Design

**Option A, prospective (Roberto's proposal).** After the pre-registration and the commit that opens the seal, engine v1, unchanged, computes the alerts for fiscal years 2024 and 2025 on a fixed date. The list stays sealed (only its SHA-256 goes into git) and is compared, on an evaluation date set in the pre-registration, with the cases that came to light by then.

**Option B, retrospective, only with the cases already public today.** The same computation, compared only with the cases public on the pre-registration date.

**Option C, A with a pre-registered split.** The cases already public on the seal date and the ones that come to light after it are counted together and also separately.

| | A, prospective | B, only cases public today | C, A with the split |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Expected detectable cases (medium rate) | 1.6 by 2029-12-31 | 0.9 today | the same as A |
| When the result comes out | on the evaluation date (years) | right after the opening | at both moments |
| Contamination risk | low: the ground truth of the new cases is written later | higher: Roberto and Claude may already know 2024 and 2025 cases | the pre-seal part is kept separate |
| What it allows concluding | whether the alert came before the case became public, with no choice made afterwards | the same, with fewer cases and with the ground truth written knowing what is already public | both, without mixing them |

The engine does not change, so knowing old cases does not tune the engine; the risk of option B is in the ground truth (which cases enter and with which period), not in the computation.

**Fixed computation date.** I propose the study 1 rule: each fiscal year in the version of the DFP public at the cut-off (last day of the fourth month after the fiscal year), 2025-04-30 for 2024 and 2026-04-30 for 2025, with the ENET versions. Both dates have passed, and the computation is reproducible. The alternative, a single date for both fiscal years, would use, for 2024, versions refiled after the cut-off and break the comparison with study 1.

**Recommendation: C**, with the computation on the study 1 cut-off dates and a single evaluation date for the primary outcome, with no look before it (section c). The secondary outcome has its own, earlier date and publishes only aggregate counts (section b).

## b. Outcome

**Primary: an accounting irregularity that comes to light**, as in study 1: a CVM sanctioning proceeding about the financial statements, fraud or error admitted in a material fact notice, or a restatement order. In study 1, the time between the end of the affected period and the public date was 12.4 months at the median in the 13 detectable cases (from 4.3 to 56.9) and 18.2 months in the 36 cases with a period and a date (from -24.4 to 118.1; the negative minimum is a case that came to light before the end of the affected period); 92% of those cases became public within 60 months. Study 1's median of 19.4 months is a different measure: it runs from the filing of the first DFP in alert to the public date, only for the 7 hits. **It allows concluding:** whether the engine alerted before the irregularity came to light, which is the product's question. With few cases, it is a descriptive check, not a statistical test (section c).

**Secondary: a material restatement of fiscal year t in the comparative of DFP t+1**, by the product's materiality rule (a difference in group (a), totals and key lines, above 1% of total assets of the version used). From 2010 to 2022, 1,407 of 8,084 company-years (17.4%) had a highlighted difference; from 2013 to 2022, the rate was 14.2% per year. That rule includes reclassifications and changes of perimeter, which do not indicate an error. A narrower definition, a highlighted difference in equity or in net income, occurred in 439 company-years from 2010 to 2022 (5.4%), with a rate of 4.7% from 2013 to 2022. The outcome shows up early: the one for 2024 is already in the comparative of the 2025 DFP (filed in 2026), and the one for 2025 will be in the 2026 DFP (by 2027-04-30). **It allows concluding:** whether the alert is associated with a later material restatement, that is, with the quality or instability of the accounting information; it does not allow talking about fraud. Size, sector, financial distress and changes of perimeter confound the association, so the comparison is with study 1's naive rule (most leveraged decile), side by side.

For fiscal year 2024, the secondary outcome already exists: the pre-registration must forbid reading the comparative of the 2025 DFP before the list is sealed.

**Recommendation:** primary as in study 1, with the CVM restatement order included; secondary by the narrow definition (equity or net income), with the product's broad rule only as descriptive, evaluated after 2027-04-30 and published only as aggregate counts. The narrow definition has fewer events and less power (section c), but it measures better what matters; the broad one has more power and more noise.

## c. Power

**How many company-years.** From the metadata, 709 companies filed the 2024 DFP (683 with the first filing by 2025-04-30) and 671 the 2025 one so far (649 by 2026-04-30); the 2023 one had 732 (701 by 2024-04-30). In study 1, about 600 non-financial companies per year were evaluated (597, 596 and 601 from 2021 to 2023). I expect close to 1,200 company-years for the two fiscal years.

**How many detectable cases to expect.** The count uses three rates of detectable cases per fiscal year, taken from study 1's ground truth: low, 0.25 (what was observed from 2016 to 2023, still incomplete, because recent cases may not have come to light yet); medium, 0.93 (2010 to 2023); high, 2.2 (2011 to 2015). It multiplies each rate by the share of study 1 cases that were already public at the same lag.

| Evaluation date | Share public (2024; 2025) | Expected, low rate | medium | high |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-15 (today) | 0.61; 0.33 | 0.2 | 0.9 | 2.1 |
| 2027-12-31 | 0.67; 0.61 | 0.3 | 1.2 | 2.8 |
| 2028-12-31 | 0.81; 0.67 | 0.4 | 1.4 | 3.2 |
| 2029-12-31 | 0.92; 0.81 | 0.4 | 1.6 | 3.8 |
| 2030-12-31 | 0.97; 0.92 | 0.5 | 1.8 | 4.2 |

**How many hits tell the engine apart from chance.** With study 1's alert rate of about 50%, each case is hit by chance with probability 0.5. With up to 4 cases, no result is distinguishable from chance at 5% significance: 4 of 4 happens by chance 6.25% of the time.

| 4 | no result is enough | 0.0625 | 0 | 0 |
| 5 | 5 | 0.0312 | 0.237 | 0.590 |
| 6 | 6 | 0.0156 | 0.178 | 0.531 |

| 10 | 9 | 0.0107 | 0.244 | 0.736 |
| 13 | 10 | 0.0461 | 0.584 | 0.966 |
| 15 | 12 | 0.0176 | 0.461 | 0.944 |
| 20 | 15 | 0.0207 | 0.617 | 0.989 |
| 30 | 20 | 0.0494 | 0.894 | 1.000 |

If a case's affected period covers both fiscal years, the chance of at least one alert by luck rises to 0.75 with independent years and to 0.87 with study 1's persistence (73.8% of the company-years in alert were also in alert the year before), and the bar gets higher.

**What the test allows concluding.** Through 2030, the expectation is 2 detectable cases or fewer at the medium rate: the primary outcome does not tell the engine apart from chance. It serves as a descriptive check, case by case, with each hit's lead time. A result like 0 of 3 is informative, but weak. The secondary outcome has more power: with 1,200 company-years, half in alert and the narrow average restatement rate of 4.7%, the power for a rate ratio of 1.5 is 0.45 and, for 2.0, 0.82; by the broad rule (14.2%), 0.88 and 1.00. The count assumes independent company-years; with two fiscal years per company, the real power is lower.

**Recommendation:** primary outcome evaluated on 2029-12-31 (60 and 48 months after the fiscal years, when 92% and 81% of study 1's cases were already public), described case by case, with no test against chance as the main criterion; the statistical test stays with the secondary outcome.

## d. Custody of the sealed list

**The file.** A CSV in a fixed format (UTF-8, LF line breaks, sorted by CVM code and fiscal year), with all evaluated company-years, in alert or not, and each one's signals; in the header, the code commit and the SHA-256 of each CVM file used. Having all company-years avoids later disputes about who was evaluated.

**The proof that it did not change (options).**
1. The SHA-256 in a commit pushed to GitHub. GitHub records the time of the push and of the CI on that commit, outside my environment. The commit date alone proves nothing, because it can be written by hand.
2. An independent timestamp of the hash (OpenTimestamps, free, or an RFC 3161 time-stamping authority).
3. Recompute the list at the evaluation: the engine is deterministic, and the list comes from the frozen code and the CVM files in the manifest (the versions at the cut-off come from ENET). So as not to depend on the CVM changing its files, the zips used are archived, with their SHA-256 in the manifest.

**Where the file lives.** Encrypted with Roberto's public key (age or GPG); only the public key enters the repository. The encrypted file goes to two places Roberto controls (for example, a private repository and a drive), and the plaintext copy is deleted from the environment, which is temporary. The private key never enters the environment.

**The limit.** The code and the data are public: anyone, including whoever writes the ground truth, can recompute the list. The seal prevents changing the list, not discovering it. The blinding of the ground truth is procedural: whoever writes it commits not to run the code, and the ground-truth criteria are objective, document-based.

**Recommendation:** options 1 and 3, plus OpenTimestamps (option 2), which costs little; the encrypted file with Roberto, in two places; the zips archived.

## e. Ground truth

**Sources.** (1) CVM sanctioning proceedings about the financial statements (accounting standards and financial information), with the date of the opening or of the first public document; (2) material fact notices that admit an accounting error or fraud (the content of IPE filings after 2024-12-31 is still not read in my environment; Roberto reads it, outside it); (3) CVM restatement or republication orders; (4) for financial companies, the Central Bank.

**Criterion**, as in study 1: `data_publica` is the first documented public date; the affected period comes from the fiscal years named in the decision or the material fact notice; a case without a document giving the years does not count as detectable. Detectable: the affected period includes 2024 or 2025, the DFP for that fiscal year was public at the cut-off and before the public date, and the company-year is in the sample. Cases without a misstatement in the financial statements (only insider trading or governance) stay out. Each case with two sources, as in study 1's amendment 9.

**Blinding.** I agree with the proposal: Roberto writes the ground truth with Claude (claude.ai), outside this environment, without access to the list and without running the code, like the reader's held-out cases. I recommend two parts: part 1, with the cases already public on the seal date, written and frozen (SHA-256 in a commit) before the seal is opened; part 2, with the cases that come to light by the evaluation date, written after it and frozen before the list is opened.

## f. Opening the seal

**What must be ready before, in this order.**
1. The study 2 pre-registration in a commit, with its hash recorded in the next commit, as in study 1 and the reader.
2. Part 1 of the ground truth frozen (SHA-256 in a commit).
3. Roberto's public key in the repository; the list format and the script that computes and seals it, frozen with the SHA-256 of each file and a test that flags a change; engine v1 unchanged (the invariant passes, and the engine files enter the hash record).
4. Only then download the 2024 and 2025 DFPs (and the ENET versions at the cut-offs), with the SHA-256 of each file in the manifest.

**The commit that opens the seal (options).**
- **Global:** `ULTIMO_EXERCICIO_LIBERADO` from 2023 to 2025 and the maximum date to 2026-04-30. It is simple, but it also opens the product: the API and the demo would start showing 2024 and 2025, and a regenerated demo would show alerts from those years for the ground-truth companies, which would publish part of the list before the evaluation.
- **Only for the study (recommended):** a study-specific constant in `chiocca/v2/lacre.py` (for example, `ESTUDO_2_EXERCICIOS = (2024, 2025)`), read only by the frozen study script. The product keeps 2023 and 2024-12-31 until the evaluation, and the monitor rule (metadata only after 2024-12-31) stays for the product. The seal test then checks that nothing but the study script reads 2024 and 2025.

After the commit, in the same session: compute the list, record its SHA-256 in a commit, encrypt the file for Roberto, deliver the encrypted file and delete the plaintext copy. Nothing else from 2024 and 2025 is computed until the evaluation dates.

## g. Improvements after the clean test

Each one with its own pre-registration, compared with engine v1 unchanged and with the naive rule, and evaluated on data that were not used to develop it (fiscal years 2026 onward or new held-out cases). Recommended order:

1. **Metrics, without changing the engine:** pre-registered lead time, lift and top precision, NDCG@k, a paired bootstrap by company, a 24-month gap and the rule for multi-year cases. I recommend that they enter the clean test's pre-registration already, as secondary metrics.
2. **Implied interest:** separate exchange-rate variation and lease interest from finance costs, through the notes. It is the largest source of alerts in study 1 (59.8% of the company-years in alert).
3. **DEPI from the DVA:** the M-score had no value in 1,548 of 6,938 company-years, and in 7 of the 13 detectable cases.
4. **Discretionary accruals** (modified Jones and the margin model, with at least 10 companies per sector and year), Dechow and Dichev with a lag and earnings just above zero.
5. **Dechow et al. (2011) F-score.**
6. **Cut-offs recalibrated for Brazil or by sector.**
7. **Quarterly data (ITR)**, for cases like IRB.
8. **Supervised learning**, exploratory only.
9. **Adjusted Benford deviation, based on Chakrabarty et al.'s (2025) AB-score, in a label-free version.** Added on 2026-11-22, after the study 2 pre-registration, which does not change; the order of items 1 to 8, cited in the pre-registration, does not change either. Reference in [`docs/en/references.md`](references.md).
   - *What:* the mean absolute deviation of the first-digit distribution of the figures in the DFP version used, normalized within the year by bins of the number of figures (the article's `B_Input`) and by the year (`B_Year`); alert in the top decile of the year, within each group.
   - *Why:* it can be computed for almost every company-year; in study 1, the M-score had no value in 1,548 of 6,938.
   - *Known limits:* the article's coefficients were estimated with US AAERs and are not used; on its own, in the article, it flags too much, so it enters as a complementary signal; the DFP has fewer figures than Compustat, and the distribution of the number of figures per company-year must be described first, only with fiscal years up to 2023.
   - *Rule:* its own pre-registration before any code, development only with fiscal years up to 2023 and evaluation on data that were not used to develop it.
